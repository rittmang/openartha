# OpenArtha product context

## Product

OpenArtha is an open, source-aware library for finding meaning in primary texts by reading the text, comparing interpretations, and following every reading to its source. Artha (AR-tha) means meaning, purpose, or sense in Sanskrit. The Bhagavad Gita is the first work.

The current product contains the 700-verse Gita reader, unified text and commentary search, 29 supplied translation/commentary fields from 22 authors, source and rights records, versioned downloads, and read-only APIs. It does not contain AI analysis or a curator chatbot.

## Audiences

- Readers who want the Sanskrit, transliteration, and English together.
- Readers and students who want to compare translations and commentaries verse by verse.
- Researchers who need provenance, review state, variants, and stable versions.
- Developers who want open downloads and a read-only API.

## Jobs to be done

1. Read or search a verse without losing its textual context.
2. Compare how translators and commentators interpret the same verse.
3. Check where a reading or translation came from.
4. Reuse a stable corpus without hidden corrections.

## Positioning

Core promise: Find meaning. Follow it to the source.

Positioning statement: OpenArtha helps people find meaning in primary texts by keeping the text, its interpretations, and the evidence together.

Proof points: 700 verses, 18 chapters, Devanagari, IAST, English, 29 supplied translations and commentaries from 22 authors, source locators, checksums, visible review status, versioned downloads, and open access.

Primary action: Read the Gita.

## Voice and constraints

- Keep the tone calm, respectful, and research-like.
- Use short sentences, familiar words, active voice, and concrete labels.
- Give each copy layer one job: headline for orientation, body for proof or explanation, and CTA/card title for action. Do not restate card titles in the section heading or repeat a headline in its supporting sentence.
- Explain specialist terms when they are necessary.
- State review limits clearly. Do not imply that the release candidate has completed human review.
- Do not claim religious authority or a definitive interpretation.
- Do not use false urgency, scarcity, popularity, or social proof.
- Do not advertise planned AI or commentary features as if they exist.
