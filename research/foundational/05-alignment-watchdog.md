# B05 — Alignment watchdog

Verdict: **GO**

Buildout: medium · Evidence yield: high · New expert data: review decisions generated by the project

## Decision summary

Rank commentary records by the probability that they are attached to the wrong canonical passage. The system is a smoke alarm, not an automatic editor: it creates a high-yield human review queue and turns necessary editorial work into a gold dataset.

## Research question and hypotheses

- **H1:** a preregistered ensemble of lexical, multilingual-semantic, sequence, and metadata signals yields higher precision among the top reviewed records than random, lowest-confidence, or single-signal ranking.
- Primary outcome: precision among the first `k` reviewed alerts, with `k` set from the available review budget.
- Secondary outcomes: expected reviewer minutes per confirmed issue, recall on seeded alignment corruptions, and calibration.

## Inputs needed

Existing: 20,300 machine-validated aligned records, canonical references, source sequence, alignment status/confidence, adjacent passages, 29 edition views, language/script metadata, checksums, and documented alignment exceptions.

New: two independent expert decisions for a bounded queue, with labels `correct`, `wrong`, `partial/span`, `ambiguous`, `paratext`, and `defer`. Adjudication remains separate.

## Scientific and statistical setup

1. Freeze an untouched confirmation set of editions or chapters before developing signals.
2. Score each record against its assigned verse, adjacent verses, chapter candidates, and global top candidates.
3. Compare baselines: random queue, current alignment-confidence order, lexical similarity, dense similarity, sequence discontinuity, and a simple learned fusion trained only on reviewed development cases.
4. Seed synthetic misalignments of known distance and type as a positive control; retain unmodified records as the real target distribution.
5. Reviewers see source text, candidate passages, and evidence but not model score or method. Measure agreement and adjudicate disagreements.
6. Report precision@k with beta-binomial or cluster-bootstrap intervals by edition, plus calibration and review-time survival curves.
7. Use adaptive review only after the original fixed test queue is complete; otherwise active learning contaminates the evaluation.

## Nature of the project

Human-in-the-loop anomaly detection and active corpus curation. The core deliverable is a prioritized queue with reasons and an immutable reviewer log.

## Outputs

- Editorial review tool or static queue.
- Human-verified alignment dataset and error taxonomy.
- Methods paper or dataset note: **“Review-budget-aware alignment auditing for a multilingual commentary corpus.”**
- Blog: **“We did not let the model fix the corpus; we measured where it helps humans look.”**
- Direct improvements to corpus quality after separately approved editorial changes.

## OpenArtha fit

Fit is excellent. OpenArtha already exposes the target label uncertainty, stable sequence, multiple witnesses, confidence, source locators, exceptions, and checksums. Reviewing alignment is required regardless, so annotation cost produces both research and editorial value.

Gaps: no confirmed error set, and different languages/content types may require different signals. The project itself creates the missing labels with controlled review.

## Minimum-build route

Generate a CSV/HTML queue offline from existing exports. Start with simple lexical and embedding scores plus sequence rules; postpone model training and app integration. Estimated effort: four to seven engineering days plus reviewer time.

## Stop conditions

Do not auto-correct. Stop claiming usefulness if top-k precision fails to exceed current-confidence ordering, reviewer agreement is too low, or alerts concentrate on a known harmless edition convention.

## Final verdict

**GO.** It combines high evidence value, necessary human work, and direct corpus improvement with modest buildout.
